Brewing guide

Good coffee at home comes down to four things: fresh beans, the right grind, the right water temperature and consistent measurements. This guide covers espresso and filter coffee.

Espresso at home

Brewly House Blend is our recommended espresso coffee. The starting recipe below works for most home espresso machines.

Espresso recipe

  • Dose: 18 grams of freshly ground coffee
  • Target yield: 36 grams of espresso (a 1:2 ratio)
  • Extraction time: 25–30 seconds
  • Water temperature: 92–94°C
  • Grind: fine, similar to table salt. Adjust if the shot runs faster than 20 seconds or slower than 35 seconds.

Steps

  1. Grind 18 grams of beans just before brewing.
  2. Distribute the grounds evenly in the portafilter and tamp level with about 15 kg of pressure.
  3. Lock the portafilter in and start the shot.
  4. Stop the shot when the espresso reaches about 36 grams in the cup.
  5. Taste it. If the shot tastes sour and runs fast, grind finer. If it tastes bitter and dry, grind coarser.

Filter coffee at home

Filter coffee suits our single-origin coffees, such as Ethiopia Yirgacheffe and Kenya Nyeri AA. The method below uses a pour-over dripper, such as a V60 or Kalita Wave.

Pour-over recipe

  • Coffee: 15 grams, medium-coarse grind (like sea salt)
  • Water: 250 grams, filtered
  • Water temperature: 93–96°C
  • Total brew time: about 2:30 to 3:30 minutes

How to prepare filter coffee

  1. Place a paper filter in the dripper and rinse it with hot water. Discard the rinse water. This removes paper taste and warms the cup.
  2. Add the ground coffee and level the bed gently.
  3. Pour 40 grams of water to wet the grounds evenly. Wait 30 seconds so the coffee can bloom and release gas.
  4. Pour the rest of the water slowly in circles, in two or three pours, keeping the water level steady.
  5. Let the water drain completely. A good brew finishes between 2:30 and 3:30 minutes.
  6. Serve right away. Filter coffee tastes best within 30 minutes of brewing.

Storing your beans

Keep beans in their sealed bag, away from light, heat and moisture. Use them within four weeks of the roast date printed on the bag. Do not refrigerate or freeze beans, because condensation damages their flavour.

Troubleshooting

My espresso tastes sour

The shot is probably under-extracted. Grind finer, or increase the water temperature within the 92–94°C range.

My espresso tastes bitter

The shot is probably over-extracted. Grind coarser, or stop the shot earlier.

My filter coffee tastes flat

Use fresher beans, check that the water is hot enough, and make sure the grind is not too fine. A fine grind slows the drain and creates a bitter cup.
